Trace3D

Turn photos of your tools into one custom Gridfinity bin. Trace as many objects as you like, from one photo or several, arrange them together, and export a single printable STL. Everything runs in this browser tab; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Step 1 of 5: add a photo to begin.

The bin size is checked against this plate live while you arrange objects in step 4, so you get warned as it grows.

1

Add photos

IN PROGRESS
  1. Lay one or more objects flat on a sheet of paper (letter, legal or A4). Keep all four corners of the sheet visible and uncovered.
  2. Photograph from above, from arm's length or farther. The app corrects for a slightly tilted camera, but distance matters for tall objects (see step 3).
  3. Add the photo below. You can add more photos at any time; each one gets its own calibration, and every object from every photo can go into the same bin.

3

Trace objects

LOCKED
  1. Click "Trace new object", then click points around one object's outline on the photo. Click the first point again (or "Finish object") to close it.
  2. Your trace is the exact cutout: nothing is added around it. Trace right along the object's edge, or slightly outside it if you want a looser fit.
  3. For tall objects, trace where the object meets the paper, not its outermost visible edge. The top of a tall object is closer to the camera, so it looks bigger than it is.
  4. Repeat for every other object in this photo. Each one gets its own pocket in the bin, with a divider between them.
  5. Have another photo? Add it and calibrate it; its objects join the same bin. When you're done, go to step 4.

4

Arrange & bin settings

LOCKED
Every traced object is shown below as its own pocket. Drag objects to move them, click one to select it, and press R (or "Rotate 90°") to turn it. "Auto-arrange" packs everything into the fewest grid cells it can. Pockets turn red when they're closer than the divider thickness. To add finger room, select an object and click "+ Thumb hole" as many times as you like. Each one is a standard 25mm circle (about an adult thumb, with room to push down and lift); drag it wherever you want it. Thumb holes move and rotate with their object, and the whole circle you see is cut.
units (×7mm) = mm

5

Preview & export

LOCKED
  1. Drag to rotate the preview, scroll to zoom, and check the pockets and dividers look right.
  2. Not happy? Go back to any step, change things, and generate again.
  3. When it looks right, download it as STL or 3MF and load it into your slicer. The 3MF opens centered on the plate you picked at the top.
